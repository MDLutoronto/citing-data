# Citing data

## Why cite data?

Citations (aka references) give credit to others for their work and ideas and allow readers to track down the original work if they choose. Citing data has not always been standard practice, especially if it is data you have collected yourself, but as data becomes more and more widely shared, proper attribution is increasingly important. Citing datasets helps them become part of the scholarly record and gives proper credit to the creator of the dataset. When the dataset has been shared (such as in a data repository), citing it allows researchers to make the connection between the your research and its underlying data, so they can build upon your research or attempt to reproduce your results. So don't forget to keep track our where you're getting your data from (as you gather it) and cite it!

## Terms of use and copyright

Before using a data source in your research, you will need to check if there are any use restrictions. While data (facts) cannot be copyrighted per se, their organization into a database/data product can be. This can apply to datasets as a whole, as well as charts and graphs created by online tools. There may also be privacy concerns related to the data itself, which would limit the ways the data can be used and shared. In addition, data sources that the University of Toronto has licensed access to may have additional restrictions on use. You should always read the terms and conditions for each data source. For more information and if you have any questions about copyright, contact the <a href="https://onesearch.library.utoronto.ca/copyright/home" target="_blank">Scholarly Communications and Copyright Office</a>.

## How to cite data

Standard citation style guides may not provide much guidance on data citation formatting. Generally, you'll want to include the following elements and them format them to match your chosen style guide as closely as possible:
- Title
- Author
- Date
- Persistent identifier such as DOI
- Distributor/repository name

The following guides can provide more detail on constructing citations for different types or sources of data:
- [How to Cite Data Correctly](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/icpsr/teaching-learning/build-your-data-skills/citing-data) (ICPSR)
- [How to Cite Data: General Info](https://libguides.lib.msu.edu/citedata) (Michigan State University). Includes APA, MLA, Chicago style
- [How to Cite Statistics Canada Products](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/12-591-x/12-591-x2021001-eng.htm) (Statistics Canada)
- [Citing our Data, Tools, Technical Documents and Research](https://www.census.gov/about/policies/citation.html) (US Census Bureau)
- [ACMLA Recommended Best Practices in Citation of Cartographic Materials](https://acmla-acacc.ca/docs/ACMLA_BestPracticesCitations.pdf) (Alberta Auringer Wood, 2012)
- [How to cite ArcGIS maps and data](https://resources.esri.ca/education-and-research/how-to-cite-arcgis-maps-and-data) (Esri Canada)
- [Citing Maps and Atlases](https://libguides.mcmaster.ca/cite-maps) (McMaster University Libraries)
